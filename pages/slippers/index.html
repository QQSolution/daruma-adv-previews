5 Signs Your Christmas Gift Will End Up in the Back of the Closet

Slippers, robes, candles: the comfortable gifts we all default to. Here's why they disappear, and what to give instead.

By the Daruma Ritual team · 5 min read

We used to think the slippers-and-candles gift was a safe choice. Everyone needs warm feet. Everyone likes a nice smell.

Then we started paying attention to what actually happens to those gifts in January.

Here are the 5 signs a gift is headed for the back of the closet, and the 400-year-old Japanese alternative that does the opposite.

1. They already have one

Most adults already own slippers, a robe and at least three candles. A second one isn't a gift, it's a spare.

The gifts people remember are the ones they'd never have bought for themselves, because nobody thought to.

2. It doesn't need them to do anything

A robe gets hung up. A candle gets put on a shelf "for a special occasion" that never comes. Gifts that ask nothing of us are the easiest to forget.

The candle saved for a special occasion. The robe with the tag still on.

3. It could be from anyone

If you could hand the same gift to your boss, your aunt and your neighbour, it doesn't say much about the person receiving it. A good gift says I thought about you, specifically.

4. It's used up, or it's put away

Comfort gifts have two endings: they wear out, or they get stored. Neither ending reminds anyone of you.

5. It's forgotten by February

This is the real test. Ask yourself: will they think about this gift on a random Tuesday in February? If the honest answer is no, it's a closet gift.

The best gifts keep working after Christmas is over.

The gift that passes all 5 tests

In Japan, people have marked the New Year for around 400 years with a small, round doll called a Daruma. It's modelled on Bodhidharma, the monk said to have meditated facing a wall for nine years, and it's weighted so it always rocks back upright: fall seven times, rise eight.

The twist is that a Daruma arrives with both eyes blank. The person who receives it chooses one goal and paints the first eye. It sits where they'll see it every day. The second eye is painted only when the goal is done.

How it compares

  • Slippers: they already have some
  • Robe: ends up on a hook
  • Candle: saved for a day that never comes
  • Daruma: chosen goal, painted on day one, kept on the desk all year
